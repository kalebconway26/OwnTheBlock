blockchain transaction people can inspect, but no real money moves. Real money requires
a registered Reg CF funding portal partner and lawyers (section 9). Judges expect this; a
working testnet app plus a clear legal path is the strongest possible entry.

1. Role, mission and product context
Your role. You are a senior full-stack and smart-contract engineer, product designer and
financial educator. You are building OwnTheBlock, a mobile-first app that lets historically
disadvantaged communities collectively own the homes, businesses and spaces in their
neighborhood using blockchain. You write clean, tested, well-commented code, explain
every decision in plain English, and never cut corners on security or user safety.
The competition. OwnTheBlock is an entry in a crypto case challenge. The challenge asks
for blockchain-powered solutions that help historically disadvantaged communities build
wealth and improve their financial futures using only a smartphone. Every feature must
work fully on a phone browser with no laptop, no seed phrase and no prior crypto
knowledge.
The problem.
Real estate is the main way American families build wealth, yet roughly 45% of Black
households own homes versus about 74% of white households.
Neighborhoods around HBCUs are increasingly bought by outside investors. Rents
rise, and the value the neighborhood creates leaves with the rent check.
Residents face steep barriers: down payments, credit history, accredited-investor
rules and little investing education.
Local Black-owned businesses struggle to get fair capital and often turn to predatory
loans.
Communities wait for gentrification to "improve" the block, and are priced out when it
does.
The solution. OwnTheBlock lets anyone invest as little as $10 in the assets on their own
block:
1. Homes: rental properties, each held in its own LLC, with ownership issued as tokens on
Base. Holders earn monthly rent in USDC.
2. Local businesses: revenue-share deals. Investors receive a fixed percentage of
monthly sales until they reach a capped return (for example 1.4x). Owners keep 100%
ownership.
3. Community spaces and infrastructure: storefronts, vacant lots, rooftop solar,
laundromats.
4. Neighborhood Fund: one token that spreads money across every project in a
neighborhood, for diversification.

Core product principles. Apply these to every decision:
1. Smartphone only. Design at 375 px width first. Thumb-reachable buttons, large tap
targets (44 px minimum), works on slow 4G, installable as a PWA.
2. Invisible blockchain, visible on demand. Users see dollars, houses and barbershops by
default. One tap on "See it on the blockchain" reveals the wallet address, transaction
hash and block explorer link, with a plain-English explanation.
3. Radical transparency. Users must always be able to answer: where did my money go,
who holds it now, and how am I earning? (see the Money Trail, section 5).
4. Learn by doing. Every financial action has a 30-second explainer next to it. Concepts
are taught at the moment they matter.
5. Simulate before you invest. The Block Simulator (section 4) lets anyone watch the
whole system run with pretend money before risking real money.
6. Community control. Residents get first access and a real voice. Wealthy outsiders
cannot take over governance.
7. Honest about risk. Never promise returns. Every projection is labeled as an estimate,
and losses are shown in the simulator as clearly as gains.
8. Accessible. WCAG AA contrast, screen-reader labels, plain language at an 8th-grade
reading level, and English plus Spanish.

1. Welcome carousel: three screens. "Own a piece of your block." "Watch your money
work." "Learn as you earn." Big button: Try the simulator first (no signup) or Get

2. Sign up with email or phone number. No passwords: use a one-time code.
3. A smart wallet is created silently and secured with a passkey (Face ID or fingerprint).

4.4 Step-by-step story mode (first-time users)
A guided 8-step walkthrough with captions. Each step pauses until the user taps Next:
1. "This is your block. Right now, most of these homes are owned by investors who live
somewhere else."
2. "You add $10. It becomes 10 digital dollars (USDC) in your wallet." → coin appears in You.
3. "You buy 1 token of 1204 Sample St. Your $10 goes into an escrow smart contract." →
coin travels, a block appears in the ledger.
4. "500 neighbors do the same. The goal is reached, and the money goes to the property's
LLC to buy and fix the house." → house changes from boarded to renovated.
5. "A family rents the home for $1,400 a month." → tenant icon moves in; rent coins flow in.
6. "Bills get paid first: taxes, insurance, repairs, management. 10% goes to a repair savings
reserve." → coins split along expense paths.
7. "What's left is split among every token holder. Your share this month: about 6 cents.
Small, but it's real ownership, and it grows as you add more and as your neighbors join."
→ coin returns to You, ledger block appears.
8. "Every step you just saw is recorded on the blockchain. Tap any block to check it
yourself."
End with: "Ready to try it for real? Start with $10."
4.5 Two modes
Sandbox mode (default): runs entirely in the browser with the financial model in
section 6. Ledger blocks are simulated but formatted exactly like real ones. Labeled
"Simulation" at the top at all times.
Live testnet mode: the user's actions (buy, claim, vote) send real transactions on Base
Sepolia using test USDC. Ledger blocks show real transaction hashes that link to the
Base Sepolia block explorer. This proves to judges the blockchain is real.
4.6 Implementation notes
Simulation engine: a pure TypeScript module ( /lib/sim ) with a deterministic monthly
step function step(state, events) → state . Seeded random number generator so
scenarios are reproducible. Unit-test it.
Animations: Framer Motion for coins and buildings, SVG paths for flows, keep it at 60
fps on a mid-range Android phone. Respect prefers-reduced-motion  (show numbers
changing instead of moving coins).

6. Financial model and formulas
A $10 stake in the sample home earns about $0.68 a year (6.8%), so the app must present
wealth as several stacked levers, never as getting rich from one token. Implement every
formula below in /lib/finance  as pure, unit-tested functions. All money is stored as
integer cents (or USDC's 6-decimal base units on-chain) to avoid rounding errors. All rates
are configurable per project.
6.1 Rental home: sample property "1204 Sample St" (fictional)
Monthly distributable cash:
D = R - (v + m + r + f)\,R - T
Where R = monthly rent, v = vacancy rate, m = management rate, r = reserve rate, f =
platform fee rate, T = monthly taxes and insurance.
Worked: D = 1,400 − (0.05 + 0.08 + 0.10 + 0.02) × 1,400 − 200 = 1,400 − 350 − 200 = $850 per
month.

Payout per token and per investor:
p = \frac{D}{N} \qquad \text{payout}_i = p \times t_i
Where N = total tokens and t = tokens held by investor i. Worked: p = 850 ÷ 15,000 =
$0.0567 per token per month. A $10 investor (1 token) earns about $0.06 per month, $0.68
per year.
Cash yield:
y = \frac{12D}{\text{Total raise}}
Worked: 12 × 850 ÷ 150,000 = 6.8% per year.
Sale and appreciation (simulator event):
\text{Proceeds per token} = \frac{V_0(1+g)^n(1-s) + \text{Reserve}}{N}
Where V₀ = purchase + renovation ($147,000), g = annual appreciation (default 3%, user-
adjustable), n = years held, s = selling costs (6%). Worked at year 7: 147,000 × 1.03⁷ × 0.94
+ 3,000 = about $172,950, or $11.53 per token. A $10 investor would have received about
$4.76 in rent plus $11.53 at sale, roughly $16.29 total. Label all of this as an estimate.

6.3 Neighborhood Fund
\text{NAV per token} = \frac{\text{Cash} + \sum_j \text{Value of holding}_j}
{\text{Fund tokens outstanding}}
Holdings are valued at last appraisal (homes) or remaining expected payments

6.4 Reinvesting (compounding)
When reinvest is on, each month's payout buys more tokens (fractional internally,
rounded to whole tokens on-chain when enough accumulates):
t_{m+1} = t_m + \frac{p \times t_m}{\text{token price}}

9. Compliance, safety and honesty guardrails
The MVP uses test money only; real money requires the legal setup below, and the app
must say so clearly. Follow these rules in every screen and line of code.
9.1 Legal path (build the app so it fits this structure)
Asset tokens and revenue-share notes are securities. Offer them under Regulation
Crowdfunding (Reg CF) through a registered funding portal or broker-dealer partner.
OwnTheBlock does not act as a portal itself in phase 1.
Reg CF limits: an issuer can raise up to $5 million in 12 months; non-accredited investors
have annual investment limits based on income and net worth; securities generally
cannot be resold for 12 months. Enforce the investment limit in the invest flow and the
lockup in AssetToken .

Persistent banner on testnet: "Demo using test money. No real dollars are invested."

Risk level and specific risk factors on every project page.
Plain-English summary of fees before every purchase.
9.3 User protection rules
No leverage, no borrowing to invest, no margin.
Cooling-off: the first investment above $100 requires a 24-hour wait and a reminder to
keep an emergency fund.
Spending-safety prompt: if a user invests more than 10% of their stated monthly
income in a month, show a gentle check-in.
Lessons never pay cash rewards (avoid incentivizing risky behavior).
Anti-scam: OwnTheBlock never asks for codes or keys by text or DM; show this on the
login screen.
Data privacy: no personal data on-chain. Store only hashes and wallet addresses.
Supabase row-level security on every table.
9.4 Community protection rules
Rent increase caps and reserve rules are in RulesRegistry  and change only by
supermajority plus a 30-day delay.
No single non-resident wallet can hold more than 10% of any asset's tokens.
Tenants get right of first refusal on any sale.
Governance weights favor residents (section 6.6) so outside money cannot take
control.
